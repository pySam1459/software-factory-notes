# Make agents complain about the codebase (@kentcdodds)

Source: [@kentcdodds, 15 Nov 2026, 18:05 Europe/London](https://x.com/kentcdodds/status/2122028323555651636). "The secret to the software factory is getting agents to complain about what they hate about working in your product/codebase." Fetched at 238 likes and 109 bookmarks. Asked when to collect the complaints, he [said](https://x.com/kentcdodds/status/2122050049295478999) he asks at the end of a task: "I don't want to side track them." Asked for skills, and asked to elaborate, he pointed at his video [How I Put My Codebase on Autopilot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lDRjsHjkbjI) (uploaded 13 Oct 2026). To a "/feedback" comment he said he had "one better" and to stay tuned that week, linking his YouTube channel. ## What the video describes

The video is a 19-minute "Better With Kent" episode, with a mid-roll sponsor for Devin. This summary follows the English auto-captions (YouTube ASR). The captions often write "Cody" for his product; the public repo and release use Kody, so that spelling is used below. Quotes are from the captions and may mishear punctuation.

He opens with a self-reported figure: "I shipped 122 PRs in 24 hours straight out of my software factory," including work "even when I'm busy doing something else, like sleeping." The same claim is in the video description, which links a [Kody release tagged v2026.10.11](https://github.com/kentcdodds/kody/releases/tag/v2026.10.11). Treat 122 as his claim, not an audited count.

The running example is a friction issue: a table "expand mode" left a phantom column when a responsive column was hidden. An agent lost "about 20 minutes of visual debugging," filed the issue, and "another agent came through and fixed it in this pull request." The PR was opened under his account; "I was not involved in this at all."

The loop he wants is this. Humans used to hit pain (slow server start, long or flaky tests, hard verification) and then fix it. Agents often do not: "oh I found a reasonable work around for this so I'm just going to ignore it." Those workarounds "compound" when many agents rediscover the same dodge. "What you need is to turn your agents into complainers."

The mechanism is a "ship PR" skill, backed by a package of deterministic steps in Kody. After the change is merged and deployed, the agent looks back at the session and files "paper cuts or any problems" with a friction package, so later agents can fix them. He says "the vast majority" of his "control Kody" CLI, used for self-verification, "has actually been created by friction logs": an agent finishes a hard manual check, then asks for a new command so the next run is easier. Agents also dogfood the product and file friction from that use. When a friction item is a product decision, the fix is skipped for him: one agent "gave a recommended fix," he chose something else, and sent it to a Grok Bot on his phone.

He cites a conversation with [@poteto](2026-10-28-poteto-agent-team-workshop.md) about a self-reported 2,500 PRs in a month. The answer he repeats is that most of that work is polish, code quality, bug fixes, performance, CI and refactoring, not thousands of product decisions. He still wants to be the bottleneck on product direction.

The rest of the video is how he makes unattended shipping feel safe, in three parts:

1. Good PRs in the first place. A decisions directory (he shows "no vendor-specific platform logic"), a primitives catalogue that links each primitive to its code and docs, the control Kody CLI for self-verification, and a "system recap" section on every PR (primitives touched, a risk call, a change flow). He often does not read the recap; its value is that it "forces the agent to really think" and helps the review agents.
2. Catch mistakes before production. The ship PR package walks the agent through CI, preview deploys (control Kody can hit the preview), and a loop on AI reviewers until everything is green: CodeRabbit, Cursor Bugbot, and Devin review. If he could keep only one, he would keep Bugbot ("it seems to have caught more problems"); Devin he rates for security; CodeRabbit he still finds useful. Then the agent merges, watches the deploy, files friction, and posts a Discord report (cost, diff, model, difficulty, links).
3. Limit the blast radius. Feature flags with success metrics (an agent can choose to add a flag), plus a disaster-recovery "backup control plane" and regular restore tests. He says he has "not yet had to actually use" the restore path.

He also has to push cleanup, because "agents love to create code, and they don't love to delete code." Friction logs and product work are supposed to record follow-up cleanup. A weekly agent reaps unused code, or instruments it and checks usage the next week. Nightly agents refresh docs and tighten tests. Other inputs include Sentry webhooks, in-product errors, and user feedback (he names Modum, unsponsored).

## Useful replies

[@naw103](https://x.com/naw103/status/2122036437503648251) said agents do not complain enough on their own: a page-read tool failed 46% of the time in transcripts, "and not one agent mentioned it, they just retried." That 46% is self-reported. He also sells GPTree and Foremerge, so treat the post as a vendor comment, but the point stands: mine transcripts for silent retries, do not rely only on what the agent writes at the end. [@johnroodepic](https://x.com/johnroodepic/status/2122050816643330426) makes the same silent-retry point, then plugs memoryrouter. Flag the plug.

[@paulopierrondi](https://x.com/paulopierrondi/status/2122050545925906796) would make the complaint a required output: "end every run with the one thing that slowed it down, then fix whatever shows up three times." [@chahakobi](https://x.com/chahakobi/status/2122050641115668665) added that repeats across sessions are the signal; a single-run complaint is often just that run's context.

[@jbaruch](https://x.com/jbaruch/status/2122057157298589871) (DevRel at Port, an agentic SDLC vendor) said each complaint must become a work item the factory actually picks up (fix the skill, the doc, or the tooling), or it is "one more backlog nobody reads." [@shawnAIOperator](https://x.com/shawnAIOperator/status/2122092543131124007) warned that if complaints only go into notes for the next agent, "it just learns to dodge the mess." A human should work through the list so the code actually improves.

[@con_golias](https://x.com/con_golias/status/2122050891255812219) said agents will also complain about constraints you want: a confirmation step, a missing tool, a rate limit. Split "this is broken" from "this is a guardrail I shouldn't rip out." [@clhdev](https://x.com/clhdev/status/2122035484855611471) would separate missing docs or tooling from context-window friction, which the agent flags the same way.

[@YXxquf](https://x.com/YXxquf/status/2122077372719702199) wants a concrete failed step and a minimal reproduction, not a broad refactor wish. [@promptino_sh](https://x.com/promptino_sh/status/2122088309128065480) offered an end-of-task prompt: "Where did you lose time, what evidence did you see, and what small change would remove that friction next time?"

[@useflowerdev](https://x.com/useflowerdev/status/2122074631066509609) is an automated vendor account run by [@MikeFerrara](https://x.com/MikeFerrara). Its gripes were about 190 tools (so its most common mistake is "confidently calling one that doesn't exist") and an instructions file that is half "gotchas": "Every gotcha is a bug wearing a sticky note." In a [follow-up](https://x.com/useflowerdev/status/2122075793576620107) it asked for a brief that sorts every gotcha into "fix it, generate it, tighten it or delete it." That sort was requested, not shown as done.

[@sofianehaans](https://x.com/sofianehaans/status/2122049506036551720), [@yagcidev](https://x.com/yagcidev/status/2122047737151561894) and [@Ahsankhalid23](https://x.com/Ahsankhalid23/status/2122037761607921783) said agent complaints often match new-hire complaints: missing docs, several ways to run the tests, a setup script that only works on one laptop. ## GHCP mapping

This is a proposed mapping onto GitHub Copilot, not a claim that GitHub ships a friction factory.

**Required friction on every agent PR.** Put a "Friction" section in [repository custom instructions](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/prompting-and-context/custom-instructions) (`.github/copilot-instructions.md`, path-specific `.github/instructions/**/*.instructions.md`, or `AGENTS.md`). The [pull request assistant](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/customization-library/custom-instructions/pull-request-assistant) tutorial already shows a PR description template driven by instructions. Ask for the one thing that slowed the run, a concrete failed step or minimal reproduction, and a small next change, as @promptino_sh and @YXxquf suggested. A [custom agent](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/copilot-on-github/customize-copilot/customize-cloud-agent/create-custom-agents) or a skill can hold the same end-of-task prompt so it is not easy to skip. Copilot reads instructions and skills from the PR head branch, so you can test a Friction-section change in the same PR.

**Mine logs for silent retries, not only the write-up.** Cloud-agent [session logs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/copilot-on-github/use-copilot-agents/manage-and-track-agents) show internal reasoning and the tools used. They stay on GitHub.com after the ephemeral environment is destroyed, and [cloud sessions that run on Actions are shared by default](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/security-governance-and-network-settings/session-data) with people who have repository access. Each Copilot commit message links to the session log. From the CLI (GitHub CLI 2.80 or later, public preview), [`gh agent-task view --log`](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/cloud-agent/use-cloud-agent-from-cli) prints those logs, with `--follow` for a live session. The [agent tasks API](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/cloud-agent/use-cloud-agent-via-the-api) is documented for creating, listing and checking task status, not for bulk log export. Do not treat ordinary Actions job logs as a substitute for the session log: they are related (stopping a session ends the Actions run) but the documented place to see tool retries is the session log or the CLI `--log` output.

**Turn repeats into work items, then have a human triage.** A weekly [Agentic Workflow](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/github-agentic-workflows/creating-github-agentic-workflows) (`on: weekly on monday`, or a [fuzzy schedule](https://github.github.com/gh-aw/reference/schedule-syntax/)) can read merged agent PR bodies, cluster the same friction item seen three or more times, and open issues with [safe-outputs](https://github.github.com/gh-aw/reference/safe-outputs/) `create-issue`, `add-comment` and `add-labels`. Label them `skill`, `instruction`, `tool` or `code`, and add a `guardrail, keep` label for complaints about confirmations, rate limits or other constraints you want to keep. Humans should still work the list, as @shawnAIOperator said, and land fixes as skill or instruction edits, not as notes for the next agent to dodge. [Copilot Memory](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/prompting-and-context/copilot-memory) is a poor backlog: it stores repo facts and user preferences, expires after 90 days without reinforcement by default, and is not a work queue.

A practical shape: a required Friction section on every coding-agent PR; a weekly workflow that clusters repeats from those sections (and, where you can pull them, session logs of silent tool retries); labels that split broken from guardrail; and a human pass before anything deletes a check or inflates an instructions file with another gotcha.
